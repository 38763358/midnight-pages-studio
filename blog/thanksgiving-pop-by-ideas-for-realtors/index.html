Thanksgiving Pop-By Ideas for Realtors

A pie, a jar of jam, a bag of apples. Small gifts, a short note and a plan for getting them to the door before the holiday rush.

Midnight Pages Studio · October 19, 2026

Thanksgiving is the easiest pop-by of the year to get right. The holiday is about gratitude, so a thank-you from you fits naturally, and it arrives before the December pile-up of cards and gifts. You don’t need a big budget. You need a small, useful gift, a tag with your name on it, and a route.

When to drop it off

Aim for the week before Thanksgiving, or the Monday and Tuesday of the holiday week at the latest. In 2026 Thanksgiving falls on Thursday, November 26. Many clients travel or host from Wednesday on, and a gift that sits on an empty porch for four days helps no one. If your list is long, split it over two weekends in mid-November.

Gift ideas that work on a doorstep

The best pop-by gifts are inexpensive, easy to carry in bulk, and useful during the holiday itself. A few that fit Thanksgiving well:

  • Pie, or the makings of one. A pie from a local bakery, or a can of pumpkin with a recipe card. “Thanks for letting me be part of your story. Here’s a slice of mine” is a line that fits.
  • Jam, honey or apple butter. Shelf-stable, looks good with a tag, and gets used over the holiday breakfast.
  • A bag of apples or pears. Cheap, seasonal, and easy to source from an orchard or farmers market.
  • Hot cider or cocoa mix. Light to carry, lasts for months.
  • A small pumpkin or gourd. Pretty on the porch, no allergy concerns.
  • Kitchen basics for the host. A dish towel, a wooden spoon, a pie server. Things that get used on Thursday.

If you bring food, keep it sealed and labeled, and leave something non-food for any client you know has allergies.

What to write on the tag

Keep it short. The gift does most of the talking; the tag just says who it’s from and why. A few lines that read naturally:

  • Grateful for clients like you.
  • Any way you slice it, I’m grateful for you.
  • Thankful for you, today and every day.
  • Gather. Give thanks. Repeat.

Add your name, phone number and email in small type at the bottom. That’s it. Skip the sales pitch; a pop-by is a thank-you, not an ad.

A tag that matches the gift looks considered rather than last-minute. If you’re handing out baked goods, Gingham Kitchen has checked picnic prints and homey icons made for anything from the kitchen. Make the route manageable

  1. Pick your list. Past clients first, then your warmest sphere: people who have referred you or would.
  2. Group by neighborhood. Plan two or three short loops instead of one long day.
  3. Prep everything the night before. Print and cut tags, tie them on, and pack the car in route order.
  4. Knock, but don’t linger. If someone answers, a two-minute chat is plenty. If not, leave the gift by the door and send a short text so it doesn’t sit outside.
  5. Note who you saw. Jot a line in your CRM for anyone you talked to. It makes the next touch easier.

A note on gift rules

Pop-bys are thank-yous to clients. Check your brokerage’s policy and any rules that apply in your state, and keep gifts separate from anything that could look like payment for a referral.
