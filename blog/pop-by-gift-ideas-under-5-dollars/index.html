Real Estate Client Gift Ideas Under $5

Most “under $5” lists stop at the gift. This one adds the cost of the bag, the twine and the tag, so the number you plan is the number you spend.

Midnight Pages Studio · November 7, 2026

A pop-by works because it is small, regular and personal. A $40 gift basket once a year is easy to forget; a $4 jar of cocoa with a handwritten line, followed by a spring seed packet and a summer popsicle, keeps your name in the kitchen. The hard part is not the idea. It is keeping the real cost per client under five dollars once the extras are counted.

Price the whole gift, not the item

When you plan, write down every line that goes into one finished gift. A simple per-client worksheet:

  • The item. Divide the bulk price by the count. A box of 24 cocoa packets is one packet per client, not one box.
  • Packaging. Cellophane bag, small kraft bag, jar or box. Often the most underestimated line.
  • Tie and filler. Twine, ribbon, shredded paper. Cheap per gift, but buy enough for the whole list.
  • The tag. Cardstock and ink. A printable file is a one-time cost spread across every gift you print from it.
  • Your time and mileage. Not a cash line, but it decides how many homes you can realistically reach.

Prices vary by region and store, so use your own receipts. Do the math once for a sample gift, multiply by your list, and you will know before you shop whether the idea fits.

20 pop-by ideas that usually fit under $5

These are items agents commonly buy in bulk. Check your local prices; the point is the category, not a promise of a price.

November and December

  1. Hot cocoa packet plus marshmallows in a cellophane bag. “Thanks a choco-lot.”
  2. Microwave popcorn with a holiday movie list. “Just popping by.”
  3. Candy canes bundled with twine. “Sweet holidays from my home to yours.”
  4. A mini pie or hand pie from a grocery bakery for Thanksgiving week.
  5. Hand warmers for cold-weather markets. “Warm thanks this season.”
  6. A tea sampler of three bags in a small envelope.

January and February

  1. A paper wall or desk calendar. Useful all year.
  2. A home-paperwork folder labeled for warranties and receipts.
  3. Lip balm for Valentine’s week.
  4. A single wrapped chocolate truffle with a short note.

Spring and summer

  1. Seed packets. Flat, light, and easy to mail if a client has moved away.
  2. A small succulent bought by the flat at a garden center.
  3. Freezer pops on a hot Saturday, delivered same day.
  4. Bubbles or sidewalk chalk for families with kids.
  5. A root beer or lemonade bottle with a summer tag.
  6. Kettle corn for the Fourth of July.

Any time

  1. A coffee sample bag from a local roaster, split from a larger bag.
  2. Goldfish crackers or another snack pack for clients with kids.
  3. A fridge magnet with useful local numbers (trash pickup, non-emergency line).
  4. A box of matches or a votive candle for a cozy-night tag.

Choose commercially packaged food over homemade when you don’t know a household’s allergies; packaged food carries an ingredient label.

Where the budget leaks

  • Packaging creep. A $1 item in a $2 gift box is a $3 gift before the tag. Bags and twine are cheaper than boxes.
  • Buying per season instead of per year. Twine, cellophane and cardstock last across several pop-bys. Buy once.
  • Custom-printed items. Branded mugs and pens carry setup fees and minimums. A printed tag on a plain item gives you the same branding for the price of paper.
  • Last-minute shopping. Seasonal aisles are cheaper and better stocked three to four weeks before the holiday.

The tag carries the brand

On a small gift, the tag is the only place your name, photo and number appear, so it does most of the work. Keep it readable: one short line, your name, phone, and either a headshot or a brokerage logo. Print at 100% on matte cardstock and trim cleanly. A crisp tag on a $2 item looks deliberate; a handwritten sticky note on a $10 item looks rushed.

If you want ready-made designs, start with the five free pop-by tags (Thanksgiving, holiday and winter lines). For December, the Christmas hot cocoa tag is one 3.5 × 3.5 inch design with a headshot slot, $3. For the first week of January, the New Year bottle hang tag is $3. If you plan a pop-by every month, the 2027 monthly tag set covers all twelve months for $9, which works out to 75 cents a design.

Assemble 50 gifts in one evening

  1. Edit the tag once. Type your name, phone and brokerage, add your photo, export a PDF.
  2. Print one test sheet on plain paper. Check the size before you use cardstock.
  3. Print the run, then trim in stacks with a paper trimmer rather than scissors.
  4. Punch every tag, cut every length of twine, then fill every bag. Doing each step for the whole batch is faster than finishing one gift at a time.
  5. Sort by route. Group addresses by neighborhood so delivery takes two or three short loops.

Keep it a thank-you

A pop-by is appreciation, not payment. Don’t tie a gift to a referral or a review, and check your brokerage’s policy and your state’s rules on client gifts. For tax planning, IRS Publication 463 limits the business-gift deduction to $25 per recipient per year, one more reason small, frequent gifts make sense. Ask your tax professional how it applies to you.
