New Year Pop-By Ideas for Realtors

Missed the December rush, or want a doorstep with less competition? January is the quiet window. Here is how to use it.

Midnight Pages Studio · November 7, 2026

December is crowded. Clients get cards from their dentist, their insurance agent and every business they bought from this year. The first two weeks of January are the opposite: the decorations come down, the mailbox empties, and people are home and thinking about the year ahead. A small New Year pop-by lands in that quiet.

December vs. January: which to choose

  • Choose December if you already have a holiday tradition clients expect, or if most of your list is local and home before mid-December.
  • Choose January if your December is packed with closings, if many clients travel over the holidays, or if you want your gift to be the only one on the porch that week.
  • Do both, lightly, by sending a card in December and doing the physical pop-by in January. That spreads the work and gives you two touches.

Good timing: from January 2 to about January 15. After mid-January, “Happy New Year” starts to sound late; switch to a winter or Valentine’s theme instead.

12 New Year pop-by ideas

  1. Sparkling cider. Non-alcoholic, works for every household. “Cheers to new keys in 2027!”
  2. A wall or desk calendar. It hangs in the kitchen all year.
  3. A home-records folder labeled for warranties, receipts and closing documents. January is when people sort paperwork for taxes.
  4. Party poppers, if you deliver before New Year’s Eve.
  5. A planner or notepad for clients who like lists.
  6. Soup mix in a jar for cold-weather markets.
  7. An ice scraper in snowy areas. Used the next morning.
  8. A smoke and CO detector battery reminder, a 9-volt or AA pack with a “fresh start” tag.
  9. Coffee or tea for the first slow mornings of the year.
  10. A small plant with “Fresh year, fresh start.”
  11. A furnace filter reminder card paired with a tiny treat.
  12. A game-day snack for the late-January playoff weekends.

Tag lines that fit January

  • Cheers to new keys in 2027!
  • Here’s to another great year in your home.
  • Fresh year, same agent. Here whenever you need me.
  • Wishing you a happy, healthy, cozy new year.
  • New year, new memories at [street name].

Keep the tag about them, not the market. “Thinking of selling?” turns a gift into a flyer. Save the market talk for when they ask.

If you give a bottle

A bottle needs a hang tag, not a square tag on twine; a tall tag loops over the neck and stays upright. Sparkling cider keeps it family-friendly and avoids any question about leaving alcohol on a doorstep. If you do choose wine, hand it to an adult rather than leaving it outside, and check local rules.

A text to send when you drop it off

Food and drinks shouldn’t sit outside in January cold. Send a quick message as you leave:

Hi [Name], happy new year! I left a little something on your porch. Hope 2027 is off to a great start at [street name].

For clients who have moved too far away to visit, send the same note by text or email as a “virtual pop-by,” and offer to recommend an agent or contractor where they live now.

Plan it in one afternoon

  1. Pick your list in December. Past clients first, then your most active referral sources.
  2. Buy supplies in late December. Post-holiday sales are a good time for cellophane, twine and cardstock.
  3. Print and assemble in one session during the slow days between Christmas and New Year.
  4. Deliver in the first two weekends of January, in short loops by neighborhood.
  5. Log every visit. Note who was home and anything they mentioned. That note is your next conversation.

What to skip in January

  • Market reports on the tag. Bring numbers only if someone asks how their home’s value is doing.
  • Unannounced evening knocks. Winter evenings are dark and early; weekend mornings and a heads-up text work better.
  • Anything perishable left outside in freezing or wet weather without telling the client it’s there.
  • Expensive gifts. Several small touches across the year do more than one big gift in January.
  • Skipping the conversation. If someone opens the door, two minutes of “How’s the house treating you?” is the real point of the visit.

Gift rules

Keep it a thank-you, not a reward for referrals, and check your brokerage’s policy and your state’s rules on client gifts.
