December Holiday Pop-By Ideas for Realtors

Cocoa, candles and something for the tree. How to plan a December pop-by that feels warm, includes everyone, and doesn’t eat your whole month.

Midnight Pages Studio · October 19, 2026

December is the busiest month for client gifts, which is both the opportunity and the problem. Your clients will get a lot of cards. A small, well-presented pop-by with a personal note stands out by being thoughtful rather than big.

Timing

The first two weeks of December are the sweet spot. Clients are home, decorating, and not yet traveling. After about December 18, many households are away or overbooked. If you missed the window, a New Year pop-by in the first week of January works just as well and has far less competition.

Gift ideas

  • Hot cocoa kit. Cocoa mix, a few marshmallows, maybe a mug. Inexpensive and easy to assemble in bulk.
  • A candle. Pairs with lines like “Wishing you a bright and cozy season.”
  • Cookies or a cookie mix in a jar. Homemade if you enjoy baking; a local bakery if you don’t.
  • An ornament. Something with your clients’ town or street on it lasts for years.
  • Coffee or tea. A small bag from a local roaster supports a neighborhood business too.
  • Wrapping supplies. A roll of tape and a pair of scissors with “Thanks for helping me wrap up a wonderful year.”
  • Sparkling cider for New Year. “Cheers to 2027” on the tag.

Wording that fits every client

Not every client celebrates Christmas. Keep a mix of tags on hand: Merry Christmas for clients you know celebrate it, and Happy Holidays or Season’s Greetings for everyone else. Lines that read well:

  • Merry everything.
  • Season’s greetings, from my home to yours.
  • Holiday calories don’t count. Referrals do.
  • Thanks for helping me wrap up a wonderful year.
  • Here’s to new beginnings.

The referral line is lighthearted; use it for clients who’ll take it that way. For everyone else, a simple thank-you is enough.

Keep December manageable

  1. Decide your list by Thanksgiving. Order supplies early; craft stores and bakeries get busy.
  2. Assemble in one session. Print, cut, tie, and box everything at once.
  3. Deliver in short loops. Two or three evenings or weekend mornings.
  4. Text when you leave something. A quick “Left a little something on your porch” keeps the gift from sitting outside.
  5. Follow up in January. A New Year’s note or newsletter keeps the conversation going after the holidays.

A note on gift rules

Check your brokerage’s policy and any state rules that apply to client gifts, and keep pop-bys as thank-yous rather than anything tied to a referral.
