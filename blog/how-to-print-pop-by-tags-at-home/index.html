How to Edit and Print Pop-By Tags at Home

Paper weight, printer settings, bleed, headshots and trimming. Everything between downloading a free pop-by tag and tying it to a gift.

Midnight Pages Studio · November 7, 2026

Free printable pop-by tags are easy to find. Getting them to look sharp on a client’s porch is where agents get stuck: the tag comes out smaller than expected, the colors print dull, the cardstock jams, or the headshot looks blurry. This guide walks through the whole job, from the file you download to the tag on the gift.

1. Know what you downloaded

Most realtor tag downloads come in one or more of these formats:

  • PDF. Ready to print. Usually locked, so you sign or stamp the tag by hand, or the text was already set by you before export.
  • PPTX. Editable in PowerPoint, Keynote or Google Slides, and importable into Canva. This is the format to use if you want your name, phone and photo printed on the tag.
  • PNG. An image you can place in Canva or any layout tool. Good for adding your own text on top.
  • Canva template link. Opens a copy in your Canva account. Convenient, but check whether it needs a paid Canva plan before you rely on it.

Look for two more details on the listing: the trim size (for example 2 × 3.5 in, or a 3.5 in square) and whether the print sheet has bleed and crop marks. Bleed is extra artwork past the cut line, usually 0.125 in, so a slightly off cut doesn’t leave a white sliver.

2. Edit it once, properly

  1. Open the PPTX in PowerPoint, Keynote or Google Slides. Google Slides is free and opens PPTX directly from Drive.
  2. Use Find & Replace for placeholders like “Your Name” and “Your Brokerage.” It changes every tag on a multi-up sheet at once.
  3. Install the fonts that came with the file before you edit. If a font is missing, the app silently swaps in another one and spacing breaks.
  4. Add your headshot if the tag has a photo slot. Use a file at least 600 pixels on the short side; a cropped screenshot from social media will print soft.
  5. Check your brokerage requirements. Many brokerages and states require the brokerage name on advertising. A tag with your phone number on it can count; ask if you’re not sure.
  6. Export to PDF. Print from the PDF, not from the editor, so what you print is exactly what you saw.

3. Pick the paper

  • Matte cardstock, 65 to 110 lb cover, is the usual range. 65 lb feeds through nearly any home printer; 80 to 110 lb feels sturdier but needs a printer that handles heavy stock.
  • Bright white keeps colors true. Kraft or cream stock looks warm but darkens the design and shifts colors.
  • Match the paper to the printer. Buy cardstock labeled for inkjet or laser, whichever you own. Some craft-store cardstock has a coating that smears.
  • Glossy photo paper makes colors pop but feels flimsy as a tag. Mount it on cardstock if you use it.

4. Set the printer

This is where most problems come from. In the print dialog:

  • Scale: 100% or Actual size. Turn off “Fit to page.” Fit to page shrinks the sheet so the tags no longer line up with the crop marks.
  • Paper size: US Letter (or whatever the PDF is set to). A Letter file on A4 paper crops the edges.
  • Paper type: Cardstock, Heavyweight or Matte. The printer slows down and lays down the right amount of ink.
  • Quality: High or Best.
  • Borderless: off. Borderless printing enlarges the page slightly. The bleed already handles the edges.
  • Feed: rear or manual tray for heavy stock, if your printer has one. A straighter paper path means fewer jams.

Then print one test sheet on plain paper. Measure a tag with a ruler. If a 3.5 inch square measures 3.5 inches, print the run on cardstock.

5. Trim, punch, tie

  1. Let inkjet prints dry for a few minutes so they don’t smudge.
  2. Cut on the crop marks with a paper trimmer. It is faster and straighter than scissors. Cut heavy cardstock one sheet at a time.
  3. Punch with a 1/8 or 1/4 inch hole punch, centered at the top or in a corner, at least 1/4 inch from the edge so it doesn’t tear.
  4. Tie with baker’s twine, jute or ribbon. Cut all lengths at once.

When to use a print shop instead

Home printing is fine for 10 to 60 tags. For a larger run, heavier stock, or two-sided tags, an office supply print counter can print the same PDF on thick cardstock and trim it for you. Bring the PDF on a USB stick, ask for 100% scale, and ask them to cut on the crop marks.

Common mistakes, quickly

  • Printing from a phone preview, which often scales the page.
  • Editing in Canva after importing a PPTX without checking fonts and spacing.
  • Printing a low-resolution headshot.
  • Skipping the plain-paper test and wasting a pack of cardstock.
  • Putting a market pitch on the tag. One warm line and your contact details are enough.
